01
How to Use This SOP
Read before touching any compensation configuration

Compensation configuration is the highest-stakes area in Workday. Errors here directly impact payroll, employee pay, and regulatory compliance. A misconfigured salary grade, plan, or eligibility rule can result in incorrect pay for hundreds of workers, audit failures, or discriminatory pay outcomes.

🔴 Critical
Compensation configuration changes that affect existing workers may trigger mass retro-pay calculations or compensation change events. Always confirm with your Payroll lead whether a config change has retrospective impact before saving in production. When in doubt — test in sandbox first, every time.
✅ Tip
Duplicate this SOP per compensation structure or review cycle. File naming convention: SOP_Comp_Grades_[Region]_v1.0_2026.html or SOP_Comp_AnnualReview_[Year].html. Keep one file per configuration object — grades, plans, and review cycles each warrant their own record.
02
Compensation Architecture Primer
The full model before you configure anything
Compensation Grade

Defines the salary band — minimum, midpoint, maximum. Typically maps to a job level or career band. Grades are currency and frequency-specific. One grade per level per market.

Grade Profile

A sub-band within a grade — used where a single grade spans multiple geographies or specialisations with different pay ranges. Allows range differentiation without creating separate grades.

Compensation Plan

The pay component — Salary, Bonus, Stock, Allowance, Car, etc. Each component is a separate plan. Workers are assigned plans based on eligibility rules. Plans drive what appears on the worker's compensation tab.

Compensation Package

A bundle of plans applied together — simplifies worker assignment. Instead of assigning 5 plans individually, assign one package. Packages drive consistency and reduce config errors.

Eligibility Rules

Conditions that determine which workers qualify for a plan or grade. Built using worker attributes (employee type, location, job family, management level). The mechanism for targeted comp design.

Compensation Review

The annual / merit cycle. A time-boxed process where managers propose changes within defined guidelines (merit %, budget pools). Requires its own cycle setup, eligibility, and guideline configuration.

Pre-Configuration Checklist
Complete before any compensation configuration
Design & Data Readiness
  • Compensation philosophy and grade structure approved by Total Rewards / C&B lead[ORG-SPECIFIC] Number of grades, band widths, spread percentages, midpoint progression confirmed
  • Currency and frequency confirmed for each grade (Annual / Monthly / Hourly)Multi-currency orgs need one grade set per currency — do not attempt to mix currencies within a single grade
  • Pay ranges sourced and approved — min, mid, max per grade per marketTypically sourced from salary survey data (Mercer, Willis Towers Watson, Radford)
  • Compensation plan inventory defined — all pay components documented (base, bonus, LTI, allowances)
  • Eligibility rules designed — which worker populations qualify for each plan
  • Compensation packages designed if applicable — which plans bundle together per role family
Stakeholder Sign-offs
  • Total Rewards / C&B team sign-off on grade structure and pay ranges
  • Finance / Payroll sign-off on frequency, currency, and pay components
  • Legal / Pay Equity review — confirm grade structure does not create discriminatory pay exposureRequired in jurisdictions with pay transparency or equal pay legislation (UK, EU, several US states)
  • Works Council notification for any changes to pay structure that affect collective agreements[ORG-SPECIFIC] — DACH, France, Netherlands, Nordics typically require notification
  • HRIS lead / Workday Compensation Admin sign-off on technical design
04
Compensation Grade & Grade Profile Setup
Configuring salary bands in Workday
1
Create the Compensation Grade
Search Create Compensation Grade. Enter the grade name per your naming convention (e.g., G05-Senior-Manager-USD). Set the currency and frequency. Add a description documenting the intended job level scope. Save — the grade exists but has no range data yet.
✅ Tip
Include currency code in the grade name. In multi-currency environments, you will have parallel grade structures (G05-USD, G05-GBP, G05-EUR). Clear naming prevents accidental assignment of a wrong-currency grade to a worker — a common and painful error to remediate.
2
Add Grade Steps / Pay Range
Within the grade, navigate to Compensation Grade Profiles or the Pay Range section. Enter Minimum, Midpoint, and Maximum values. Workday calculates the spread automatically. Verify: Spread % = (Max − Min) / Min × 100. Confirm this matches your C&B design document before saving.
⚠️ Warning
Saving a grade range update immediately recalculates compa-ratios and range penetration for all workers assigned to that grade. This can trigger alerts to managers and HR Partners. Coordinate with your HR Ops team before updating ranges on grades with existing worker assignments.
3
Create Grade Profiles (if applicable)
If your design requires sub-ranges within a grade (e.g., same grade level but different pay ranges for London vs. regional UK), create Compensation Grade Profiles. Navigate to Create Compensation Grade Profile. Link the profile to the parent grade. Set the profile-level min/mid/max. Profiles override the parent grade range for workers assigned to the profile.
💡 Rationale
Grade Profiles allow geographic or specialisation-based pay differentiation without proliferating top-level grades. Fewer grades = simpler maintenance. Use profiles aggressively in global orgs with cost-of-living or market differentials within a single job level.
4
Assign Grades to Job Profiles
Navigate to the relevant Job Profile and add the compensation grade in the Compensation section. This links the grade to the job profile — when a worker is hired or transferred into a position using this job profile, the grade is defaulted. Confirm the correct grade is mapped before completing any job profile updates.
📋 Note
Grade assignment on Job Profiles is a default — HR Partners can override at the individual worker level during a Hire or Change Job transaction. Governance controls (condition rules on the Hire/Change Job BP) should restrict how far from the default grade a worker can be placed.

Setting up salary, bonus, allowance, and LTI plans

1
Create the Compensation Plan
Search Create Compensation Plan. Select the plan type. Name per convention (e.g., SAL-Annual-USD, BONUS-Annual-Executive). Set the currency, frequency, and compensation category. Link to the relevant compensation grade if applicable (salary plans).
2
Configure Plan Rules & Defaults
Set the default amount or percentage if applicable. Configure rounding rules (nearest $100, nearest $1,000 — align with your C&B policy). Set proration rules for mid-year hires or terminations. For bonus plans, set the target % per eligibility segment.
⚠️ Warning
Proration rules on bonus and merit plans must be validated with Payroll. Incorrect proration logic on an annual bonus plan can result in incorrect payout calculations for all mid-year joiners — a high-volume, high-sensitivity error category.
3
Create Compensation Package (if applicable)
Search Create Compensation Package. Name the package per role family or level (e.g., PKG-Executive-USD). Add the relevant plans: Salary + Bonus + LTI + Car Allowance. Set the default grade. The package becomes the single assignment object used on Hire and Change Job transactions — simplifying HR Partner experience and reducing plan assignment errors.
06
Eligibility Rules
Controlling which workers qualify for which plans and grades

Eligibility rules are the gatekeeping mechanism for compensation plans. They ensure the right workers are on the right plans — and that plans don't accidentally apply to ineligible populations (e.g., a contractor on an executive bonus plan).

1
Create the Eligibility Rule
Search Create Eligibility Rule. Name descriptively (e.g., Elig: Full-Time Employees — EMEA — Exempt). Select the criteria type: Include (who qualifies) or Exclude (who does not). Build the condition using worker attributes: Employee Type, Location, Job Family, Management Level, Hire Date, etc.
✅ Tip
Build eligibility rules using Exclude logic where possible — it is more durable. Excluding "Contingent Workers" from a bonus plan is more stable than including specific employee types, because new employee types added in future will be automatically excluded unless explicitly included.
2
Assign Eligibility Rule to Plan
Navigate to the Compensation Plan → Eligibility Rules section. Add the eligibility rule. A worker must satisfy the eligibility rule to be assigned the plan during a Hire, Change Job, or Compensation Change transaction. Test with representative workers from each eligibility segment before activating.
🔴 Critical
If an eligibility rule is too broad, workers may be offered plans they should not have access to during HR transactions. This has been the source of accidental executive plan assignment to non-executive workers in multiple Workday environments. Review eligibility rule logic with Total Rewards before attaching to any plan.
3
Test Eligibility with Representative Workers
Navigate to View Compensation Eligibility for Worker. Enter test workers from each target population and confirm: (a) eligible workers see the plan, (b) ineligible workers do not. Test edge cases: workers on leave, part-time workers, recent transfers, and employees approaching tenure thresholds.
07
Annual Review Cycle (Compensation Review)
Configuring and running the annual merit and bonus review process
1
Create the Compensation Review
Search Create Compensation Review. Enter the cycle name (e.g., Annual Merit Review 2026). Set the review period dates and the effective date for changes. Select the compensation plans in scope (Merit, Bonus, etc.). Assign the security groups that will participate (managers, HR Partners, HR leadership for budgets).
2
Configure Budget Pools
Define the budget pool structure: total merit budget as a percentage of eligible payroll. Set budget allocation by org level — top-level budget distributed to business unit leaders, who allocate downward. Configure whether managers see individual budgets or only their team total. Set the budget alert threshold — warning when a manager exceeds or falls below guideline.
⚠️ Warning
Budget pool configuration must be validated with Finance before the review cycle opens. Incorrect budget amounts visible to managers — even in a "test" cycle that accidentally goes live — can create significant HR and trust issues. Confirm budget figures are final and approved before activating the cycle.
3
Configure Merit Guidelines
Set up the guideline matrix: merit increase percentage recommendations based on performance rating and compa-ratio position. Workday surfaces guidelines as reference ranges to managers during the review — not hard limits unless you configure enforcement. Set whether guidelines are advisory only or enforce min/max boundaries on manager inputs.
💡 Rationale
Guideline matrices drive pay equity outcomes — workers below the midpoint with strong performance should receive higher merit increases to move toward market. Hard enforcement of guidelines in Workday reduces pay equity risk and simplifies manager decision-making. Discuss enforcement approach with your Total Rewards lead before configuring.
4
Set Review Eligibility
Configure which workers are included in the review cycle. Standard exclusions: workers hired after a defined date (e.g., after Oct 1 for a Jan 1 effective date), workers on performance improvement plans, contingent workers, workers in specific countries with separate review processes. Use eligibility rules (Section 06) to define inclusions and exclusions precisely.
5
Launch & Monitor the Review
Set the cycle status to In Progress. Monitor via Compensation Review Dashboard — track completion rates by org, budget utilisation, and guideline exceptions. Send reminder notifications to managers with incomplete submissions before the cycle close date. Close the cycle, review and approve the proposals, then activate the effective-dated compensation changes.
📋 Note
Compensation changes from the review cycle are staged as proposals until the cycle is closed and approved. They do not flow to payroll until the cycle is approved and the effective date is reached. Confirm your payroll cutoff dates with Payroll before setting the cycle effective date.

Rollback & Risk Notes
Reversing compensation changes and managing critical risks
🔴 Critical
Compensation changes that have been paid out through payroll cannot be rolled back in Workday. The only remediation is a payroll correction in the next pay cycle. This makes pre-production testing and Finance/Payroll sign-off non-negotiable for any configuration change with payroll impact.
Risk Scenarios
RiskSeverityMitigation
Grade range updated — incorrect compa-ratios displayed to managers during reviewCriticalFreeze grade range changes during active review cycles; sequence: close cycle → update ranges → open next cycle
Eligibility rule too broad — ineligible workers assigned executive planCriticalTotal Rewards sign-off on all eligibility rules; test with ineligible personas before attaching to plan
Bonus plan proration error — incorrect payouts for mid-year hiresHighPayroll validation of proration logic in sandbox with representative test cases before production activation
Pay equity exposure from grade structure designHighLegal and Total Rewards review of grade structure before launch; run pay equity analysis post-assignment
Review cycle effective date conflicts with payroll cutoffHighConfirm payroll calendar before setting review effective dates; include Payroll lead in cycle planning
